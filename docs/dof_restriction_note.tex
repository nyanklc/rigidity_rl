\title{\bf A Node-wise DOF restriction in the Extended Bearing Rigidity Matrix}
\author{Noyan Erdin Kilic}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The unified framework of \cite{michieletto2021} builds the extended bearing
rigidity matrix as $\mathbf B^+_{\mathcal G}=[\,\Dp\mathbf U\Ebar^\top\ \
\Da\mathbf V\Ebaro^\top\,]$, with $\mathbf U$ and $\mathbf V$ indexed by edges,
and characterises infinitesimal bearing rigidity by a rank condition. Working
with heterogeneous formations we found that the edge-indexed factor cannot
carry the motion constraints of both endpoints of an edge when those endpoints
lie in different manifolds. We make this precise: requiring $\mathbf U_{ij}$ to
be faithful to the bearing differential and to annihilate the motions the two
agents cannot perform forces $\Dp^{(k)}(S_i-S_j)=0$, which for a planar and a
spatial agent holds only when the two are vertically aligned. Because the
matrix is only determined on the admissible subspace, the choice made off that
subspace does not affect the differential, but it does affect the rank, and the
rank is what the rigidity condition compares. We give a four-agent formation on
which the resulting test reports a rigid framework as flexible, propose a
node-indexed form that removes the difficulty, and show that it reproduces the
homogeneous construction of \cite{michieletto2021} exactly, so that no result
for homogeneous formations is affected. All statements are checked against a
finite-difference Jacobian of the bearing map.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

We came to this while using the framework of \cite{michieletto2021} to study
topology design for formations whose agents live in different manifolds, for
instance ground vehicles constrained to a plane together with aerial platforms
in $\SEthree$. In that setting the rank condition of Theorem~2 in
\cite{michieletto2021} sometimes reported frameworks as flexible that are in
fact rigid, and the reason turned out to be structural rather than numerical.

Everything concerning homogeneous formations, in particular Table~I and Section~V of
\cite{michieletto2021}, is unaffected: the form proposed here reduces to it
identically. The difficulty appears only for heterogeneous formations on
incomplete graphs. Both case studies in \cite{michieletto2021} use complete
graphs, where the rank condition holds trivially, so no conclusion drawn there
changes either.

We believe that The general form of (10) in [1] cannot, in general, extend heterogeneous endpoint constraints off the admissible variation space in a way that simultaneously preserves the endpoint constraints and the null-space structure required by the rank argument of Theorem 2.

\section{Setting}

We follow the notation of \cite{michieletto2021}. A formation of $n\ge3$ agents
is a framework $(\mathcal G,\chi)$ with $\mathcal G$ a directed graph with $m$
edges and $\chi\in\bar{\mathcal D}=\prod_i\mathcal D_i\subseteq\SEthree^n$.
Agent $i$ has position $p_i$, attitude $R_i$, and $c_i=\dim\mathcal D_i$
degrees of freedom; write $c=\sum_ic_i=\dim\bar{\mathcal I}$. An edge
$e_k=(v_i,v_j)$ carries the bearing $b_{ij}=R_i^\top\hat p_{ij}$ with
$\hat p_{ij}=(p_j-p_i)/\lVert p_j-p_i\rVert$ and
$s_{ij}=\lVert p_j-p_i\rVert^{-1}$. Differentiating,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diff}
  \dot b_{ij}
  =\underbrace{s_{ij}R_i^\top P(\hat p_{ij})}_{\Dp^{(k)}}(\dot p_j-\dot p_i)
  +\underbrace{\bigl(-R_i^\top[\hat p_{ij}]_\times\bigr)}_{\Da^{(k)}}(-\omega_i),
\end{equation}
with $P(x)=I_3-xx^\top$ and $\omega_i$ the world-frame angular velocity. With
the incidence matrices of \cite{michieletto2021} and
$\Ebar=\mathbf E\otimes I_3$, $\Ebaro=\mathbf E_o\otimes I_3$, one has
$(\Ebar^\top x)_k=x_j-x_i$ and $(\Ebaro^\top x)_k=-x_i$.

For each agent let $S_i$ and $P_i$ be the orthogonal projectors onto the
translational and rotational directions it can realise:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
$\mathcal D_i$ & $c_i$ & $S_i$ & $P_i$\\
\midrule
$\R^2$            & $2$ & $\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)$ & $0$\\
$\R^3$            & $3$ & $I_3$                        & $0$\\
$\R^2\times S^1$  & $3$ & $\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)$ & $e_3e_3^\top$\\
$\R^3\times S^1$  & $4$ & $I_3$                        & $vv^\top$\\
$\SEthree$        & $6$ & $I_3$                        & $I_3$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Here $v\in S^2$ is the controllable axis of an $\R^3\times S^1$ agent, and
$\rank S_i+\rank P_i=c_i$. Following (8) of \cite{michieletto2021} a variation
$\delta$ is lifted to $\delta^+\in\R^{6n}$ by zero padding. Write
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal A=\{\delta^+:\delta\in\bar{\mathcal I}\}
  =\range\bar S\oplus\range\bar P\subseteq\R^{6n},
\end{equation}
with $\bar S=\operatorname{blkdiag}(S_i)$, $\bar P=\operatorname{blkdiag}(P_i)$,
and let $A\in\R^{6n\times c}$ have orthonormal columns spanning $\mathcal A$.
We call $\mathcal A$ the admissible subspace and $\mathcal A^\perp$ the virtual
subspace, of dimension
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qv}
  q_v:=6n-c .
\end{equation}

\section{The two forms}

Proposition~2 of \cite{michieletto2021} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:peredge}
  \Bper_{\mathcal G}=\bigl[\ \Dp\,\mathbf U\,\Ebar^\top\quad
                             \Da\,\mathbf V\,\Ebaro^\top\ \bigr],
  \qquad
  \mathbf U=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf U_{ij}),\
  \mathbf V=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf V_{ij}).
\end{equation}
We will compare it with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pernode}
  \Bnode_{\mathcal G}=\bigl[\ \Dp\,\Ebar^\top\,\bar S\quad
                              \Da\,\Ebaro^\top\,\bar P\ \bigr].
\end{equation}
Acting on $\delta^+$, the $k$th row block of each is
\begin{align}
  (\Bper\delta^+)_k&=\Dp^{(k)}\mathbf U_{ij}(\delta_{p,j}-\delta_{p,i})
                     -\Da^{(k)}\mathbf V_{ij}\delta_{a,i},\label{eq:actper}\\
  (\Bnode\delta^+)_k&=\Dp^{(k)}(S_j\delta_{p,j}-S_i\delta_{p,i})
                     -\Da^{(k)}P_i\delta_{a,i}.\label{eq:actnode}
\end{align}
In \eqref{eq:actper} a single matrix multiplies the column blocks of both
endpoints. In \eqref{eq:actnode} each endpoint carries its own projector. For a
homogeneous formation the two coincide, since $S_i=S_j$.

\section{The matrix is determined only on \texorpdfstring{$\mathcal A$}{A}}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:underdet}
Relation (9) of \cite{michieletto2021} determines
$\mathbf B^+_{\mathcal G}$ on $\mathcal A$ and imposes nothing on
$\mathcal A^\perp$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\delta^+$ ranges over $\mathcal A$, so (9) fixes
$\mathbf B^+_{\mathcal G}\rvert_{\mathcal A}$. Entries acting on
$\mathcal A^\perp$ are multiplied by zero.
\end{proof}

Both \eqref{eq:peredge} with Table~III of \cite{michieletto2021} and
\eqref{eq:pernode} therefore satisfy Definition~13, and both reproduce
\eqref{eq:diff} on admissible variations. They differ only on
$\mathcal A^\perp$. That would be immaterial if only the differential were
used, but Theorem~2 compares ranks, and the rank sees $\mathcal A^\perp$.

The text preceding Theorem~2 in \cite{michieletto2021} supplies the missing
condition: the extended matrix differs from the ordinary one ``because of the
emergence of zero columns in correspondence of zero entries of the vector
$\delta^+$'', and $q_v$ ``corresponds to the number of null columns''. Stated
without reference to a coordinate frame, the requirement is that the matrix
vanish on the virtual subspace. We record the three properties the proof of
Theorem~2 uses.

\begin{itemize}
\item[(R1)] $\mathbf B^+_{\mathcal G}A$ equals the differential \eqref{eq:diff}
  restricted to admissible variations.
\item[(R2)] $\mathbf B^+_{\mathcal G}(I-AA^\top)=0$.
\item[(R3)] $q_v$ is the same for $\mathcal G$ and for the complete graph
  $\mathcal K$, so that it cancels between
  $\rank\mathbf B^+_{\mathcal G}=6n-q_v-q_i$ and
  $\rank\mathbf B^+_{\mathcal K}=6n-q_v-q_t$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:aligned}
(R2) is equivalent to ``every inadmissible coordinate indexes a null column''
only when $\mathcal A$ is spanned by coordinate directions. This holds for
$\R^2$, $\R^3$, $\R^2\times S^1$ and $\SEthree$, and for $\R^3\times S^1$ under
the parametrisation of Table~I, which stores $\dot\theta$ in the third slot of
$\delta_{a,i}$ and maps it through $\mathbf V_{ij}=[\,0_{3\times2}\ v\,]$. It
does not hold if one instead writes $P_i=vv^\top$ and keeps $\omega_i$ itself as
the variable, since $vv^\top$ has rank one but no zero column unless $v$ is a
coordinate axis. The two parametrisations give the same rank and the same
$q_v$; only the column-counting phrasing is sensitive to the choice, which is
why we state (R2) as an identity.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:qv}
Definition \eqref{eq:qv} takes $q_v$ to be structural rather than a count of
null columns of $\mathbf B^+_{\mathcal G}$, and (R3) then holds by
construction. The two disagree on sparse graphs: if agent $i$ is isolated in
$\mathcal G$, all six of its columns vanish, including the $c_i$ admissible
ones, and those extra zeros are infinitesimal variations of $\mathcal G$
belonging to $q_i$.
\end{remark}

\section{An edge-indexed factor cannot satisfy (R1) and (R2)}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:impossible}
Let $e_k=(v_i,v_j)$ and suppose \eqref{eq:peredge} satisfies (R1) and (R2) on
the column blocks of $i$ and $j$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obstruction}
  \Dp^{(k)}(S_i-S_j)=0,
  \qquad\text{that is}\qquad
  \range(S_i-S_j)\subseteq\vspan\{\hat p_{ij}\}.
\end{equation}
If $S_i\ne S_j$ this fails outside a set of configurations of measure zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $U=\mathbf U_{ij}$. By \eqref{eq:actper} the column block of $\Bper$ in
row block $k$ is $-\Dp^{(k)}U$ for agent $i$ and $+\Dp^{(k)}U$ for agent $j$.
Comparing with \eqref{eq:diff} on admissible directions, (R1) gives
\begin{equation*}
  \Dp^{(k)}US_i=\Dp^{(k)}S_i,\qquad \Dp^{(k)}US_j=\Dp^{(k)}S_j,
\end{equation*}
and (R2) applied to the inadmissible directions of each endpoint gives
\begin{equation*}
  \Dp^{(k)}U(I_3-S_i)=0,\qquad \Dp^{(k)}U(I_3-S_j)=0 .
\end{equation*}
Adding the two conditions for $i$ yields $\Dp^{(k)}U=\Dp^{(k)}S_i$, and those
for $j$ yield $\Dp^{(k)}U=\Dp^{(k)}S_j$; subtracting gives
\eqref{eq:obstruction}. Since $\Dp^{(k)}=s_{ij}R_i^\top P(\hat p_{ij})$ with
$s_{ij}\ne0$ and $R_i$ orthogonal,
$\ker\Dp^{(k)}=\vspan\{\hat p_{ij}\}$, which gives the second form. If
$S_i\ne S_j$ then $S_i-S_j$ is a nonzero symmetric matrix and
\eqref{eq:obstruction} confines its range to a single line, a nontrivial
algebraic condition on $(p_i,p_j)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:tableIII}
For $i$ planar and $j$ spatial, $S_i-S_j=-e_3e_3^\top$ and
\eqref{eq:obstruction} reads $P(\hat p_{ij})e_3=0$, so the two agents must be
vertically aligned. Table~III of \cite{michieletto2021} takes
$\mathbf U_{ij}=I_3$ on such edges, which satisfies (R1) but not (R2): the
column of agent $i$ for its $z$ coordinate is $-\Dp^{(k)}e_3\ne0$, although a
planar agent cannot translate in $z$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Consequences}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:graphdep}
Under \eqref{eq:peredge} with $\mathbf U_{ij}=I_3$ on mixed edges, the
$z$ column of a planar agent vanishes exactly when that agent is incident to no
spatial agent. The number of null columns therefore depends on $\mathcal E$,
and (R3) fails.
\end{proposition}

Since $q_v$ no longer cancels between $\mathcal G$ and $\mathcal K$, the
equivalence in the proof of Theorem~2 of \cite{michieletto2021} does not go
through. The following formation shows that the conclusion can fail as well.

\begin{example}\label{ex:counter}
Let $n=4$ with $\mathcal D_1=\mathcal D_2=\mathcal D_3=\R^2$ and
$\mathcal D_4=\R^3$, so $c=9$ and $q_v=15$, at
\begin{equation*}
  p_1=(0,0,0),\quad p_2=(1,0,0),\quad p_3=(1,1,0),\quad p_4=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,1),
  \qquad R_i=I_3 ,
\end{equation*}
and let $\mathcal G$ have the four edges
$\{(1,2),(1,3),(2,4),(3,4)\}$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccl}
\toprule
& $\rank\mathbf B_{\mathcal G}$ & $\rank\mathbf B_{\mathcal K}$ & conclusion\\
\midrule
edge-indexed \eqref{eq:peredge} & $6$ & $8$ & flexible\\
node-indexed \eqref{eq:pernode} & $6$ & $6$ & rigid\\
finite differences on $\bar{\mathcal I}$ & $6$ & $6$ & rigid\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The last row differentiates the bearing map along admissible variations and
uses neither construction. The framework admits two translations and a uniform
scaling and nothing else, so $c-q_t=9-3=6$ is full rank on
$\bar{\mathcal I}$; $\mathcal G$ attains it with four edges and is minimally
rigid.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
For $\mathcal K$ in Example~\ref{ex:counter} the edge-indexed matrix has
$\rank=8$, and deleting the three $z$ columns of the planar agents returns it
to $6$. Those columns carry two dimensions of rank that correspond to no
admissible motion. One combination of them is a null direction, namely the
global $z$ translation, which the planar agents cannot perform. The same effect
appears in the case study of Section~VI-B of \cite{michieletto2021}: there
$q_t=5$ is reported while the accompanying list of trivial motions has four
entries, the difference being the global $z$ translation of a formation whose
unicycles are confined to a plane. The parenthetical remark that columns
$3,6,9,10,11$ are linearly dependent on the remainder is true of each column
separately, but the three $z$ columns are not jointly removable: deleting them
lowers the rank from $13$ to $11$.
\end{remark}

\section{The node-indexed form}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:repair}
$\Bnode_{\mathcal G}$ satisfies (R1), (R2) and (R3), and for every
$\mathcal G$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
  \rank\Bnode_{\mathcal G}=6n-q_v-q_i,
  \qquad q_i=\dim\ker(\Bnode_{\mathcal G}A).
\end{equation}
Consequently $\rank\Bnode_{\mathcal G}=\rank\Bnode_{\mathcal K}$ holds if and
only if $q_i=q_t$, that is, if and only if $(\mathcal G,\chi)$ is IBR.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(R1) follows by substituting \eqref{eq:actnode} and using
$S_i\delta_{p,i}=\delta_{p,i}$, $P_i\delta_{a,i}=\delta_{a,i}$ for
$\delta^+\in\mathcal A$. For (R2), $\bar S$ and $\bar P$ are the orthogonal
projectors onto the admissible subspace, so
$\Bnode(I-AA^\top)=0$; this depends only on the agents' manifolds and not on
$\mathcal E$, which is (R3) with \eqref{eq:qv}.

For \eqref{eq:identity}, (R2) gives
$\Bnode_{\mathcal G}=\Bnode_{\mathcal G}AA^\top$, hence
$\range\Bnode_{\mathcal G}=\range(\Bnode_{\mathcal G}A)$ and
\begin{equation*}
  \rank\Bnode_{\mathcal G}=\rank(\Bnode_{\mathcal G}A)
  =c-\dim\ker(\Bnode_{\mathcal G}A)=(6n-q_v)-q_i .
\end{equation*}
The same applies to $\mathcal K$ with $q_t=\dim\ker(\Bnode_{\mathcal K}A)$, and
$q_v$ is the same constant in both. By Theorem~1 of \cite{michieletto2021},
$\ker\mathbf B_{\mathcal K}\subseteq\ker\mathbf B_{\mathcal G}$, so equality of
dimensions is equality of the two kernels, which is Definition~12 of
\cite{michieletto2021}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:homog}
If $\mathcal D_i=\mathcal D$ for all $i$ and $\mathbf U_{ij}=S$,
$\mathbf V_{ij}=P$ are the Table~I entries, then
$\Bper_{\mathcal G}=\Bnode_{\mathcal G}$ for every graph and configuration.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$(\mathbf U\Ebar^\top x)_k=S(x_j-x_i)=Sx_j-Sx_i=(\Ebar^\top\bar Sx)_k$ since
$\bar S=I_n\otimes S$, and likewise
$(\mathbf V\Ebaro^\top y)_k=-Py_i=(\Ebaro^\top\bar Py)_k$.
\end{proof}

For $\R^3\times S^1$, Proposition~\ref{prop:homog} is read with
$\mathbf V_{ij}$ in the projector parametrisation of
Remark~\ref{rem:aligned}; the two parametrisations differ as matrices but not
in rank or in $q_v$.

In implementation terms the change is to move $\mathbf U$ and $\mathbf V$ from
between $\Dp,\Da$ and the incidence matrices to the far right, as node-indexed
block-diagonal projectors. Per row block $k=(i,j)$, with each index naming the
top-left corner of a $3\times3$ block,
\begin{align}
  \Bnode[\,3k,\ 3j\,]      &\mathrel{+}=\Dp^{(k)}S_j, \notag\\
  \Bnode[\,3k,\ 3i\,]      &\mathrel{-}=\Dp^{(k)}S_i, \\
  \Bnode[\,3k,\ 3n{+}3i\,] &\mathrel{+}=\Da^{(k)}P_i. \notag
\end{align}
The two dense $3m\times3m$ factors $\mathbf U$ and $\mathbf V$ are no longer
needed.

\section{Numerical checks}

The reference in what follows is the Jacobian of the bearing map with respect
to admissible variations, obtained by central differences on the manifold, with
attitudes updated as $R_i\mapsto\exp([\varepsilon\omega_i]_\times)R_i$. It uses
neither construction.

Over $432$ frameworks covering the five manifolds at $n=3,4,6,9$ and sixteen
heterogeneous mixes at four graph densities each, with randomised
$\R^3\times S^1$ axes, \eqref{eq:pernode} reproduces this Jacobian on
$\mathcal A$ to a worst relative error of $1.1\times10^{-9}$. So does
\eqref{eq:peredge}, as Lemma~\ref{lem:underdet} requires, once each form is
read in its own parametrisation of the $\R^3\times S^1$ rotational coordinate
(Remark~\ref{rem:aligned}). The identity $\Bnode(I-AA^\top)=0$ holds to
$2.2\times10^{-16}$.

Over $1200$ random heterogeneous frameworks with $n\in\{4,5,6\}$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & edge-indexed & node-indexed\\
\midrule
\eqref{eq:identity} violated                     & $517/1200$          & $0/1200$\\
rank condition disagrees with IBR                & $81/1200$ $(6.8\%)$ & $0/1200$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For homogeneous formations the two constructions agree bitwise, checked over
$25$ graphs in each manifold at $n=3,4,6,8,12$, as
Proposition~\ref{prop:homog} predicts. The
homogeneous ranks match the closed forms $2n-3$, $3n-4$, $3n-4$, $4n-5$,
$6n-7$ at $n=3,4,6,8,12,16$, and for a heterogeneous mix
$\rank\mathbf B_{\mathcal K}\le c-q_t$ holds in every case tested. Reproducing
the case study of Section~VI-B of \cite{michieletto2021} with
\eqref{eq:peredge} returns $\rank\mathbf B^+_{\mathcal K}=13$ with six null
columns, the values reported there, which indicates that the difference lies in
the construction and not in our implementation of it. Deleting the three $z$
columns of the unicycles there lowers the rank from $13$ to $11$, and of the
five motions listed as trivial only four are admissible.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection*{Where the ambiguity comes from}

For a homogeneous planar formation, $\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)$ serves two
purposes at once. It restricts the admissible translations, and it records that
a bearing between two coplanar agents has no $z$ component, so that the
measurement manifold is $S^1\subset S^2$. Table~I is consistent with both
readings and nothing separates them.

They separate as soon as $S_i\ne S_j$. On an edge between a planar and a
spatial agent the bearing does have a nonzero $z$ component, so the
measurement reading gives $\mathbf U_{ij}=I_3$, which is the Table~III entry,
while the variation reading asks for two different restrictions at the two
endpoints. The proof of Proposition~2 in \cite{michieletto2021} describes
$\mathbf U$ as embedding the variation of each bearing in $S^2$, which is the
first reading, whereas the rank accounting behind Theorem~2 needs the second.
Separating the two is what \eqref{eq:pernode} does (Theorem~\ref{thm:repair}): $\Dp^{(k)}$ already carries
the measurement geometry, and $\bar S,\bar P$ carry the motion constraints.

\subsection*{Possible next steps}

The observation is small on its own. It becomes more interesting in what it
enables, and we would be glad to hear whether any of the following seems worth
pursuing jointly.

Minimality is the first candidate. The counting arguments that give the minimum
number of edges for rigidity in $\R^d$ rely on every edge contributing the same
rank. Once endpoints carry different constraints the per-edge contribution
varies with the pair of manifolds, and the corresponding bounds for
heterogeneous formations do not appear to be settled.

The second is the design problem that led us here: choosing which bearings to
maintain in a heterogeneous formation so that the framework is rigid with as
few measurements as possible. Sparsity alone underdetermines the answer, since
many edge sets of the same minimal size are rigid at the same configuration and
differ considerably in the rigidity eigenvalue.
